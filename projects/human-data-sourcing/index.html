Human-Data Vendor Sourcing

How I'd decide which labeling vendor gets the next batch of AI training data, using quality, delivery, cost, and whether the data actually improves the model.

The problem

A research team needs pairwise preference labels for agentic tool-use transcripts: "which response used tools better, A, B, or a tie?" Three vendors labeled 3,600 tasks over three weeks. Before the next batch, the questions are: who gets the work, at what price, and what needs fixing?

How I measured each vendor

QuestionMetricWhy it matters
Are the labels right?Accuracy on gold (known-answer) tasks, with a 95% intervalSmall gold sets are noisy, so I show the range, not just the number.
Are the labels consistent?Cohen's κ on tasks labeled twiceLow agreement means unclear guidelines or low effort, even when spot checks pass.
What does it really cost?Cost per accepted taskPrice ÷ acceptance rate. The sticker price hides rework.
Is it on time?Share of days at or above the daily targetResearch timelines depend on steady delivery.
Is the data worth buying?Model eval points gained per $1,000The point of buying data is a better model, not more labels.

These roll up into a weighted scorecard (quality 50%, delivery 25%, cost 25%), similar to supplier scorecards I've defined in sourcing work.

What I found

  • The cheapest vendor wasn't the best buy. Vendor Alpha had the lowest cost per accepted task ($4.34), but annotator agreement was low (κ 0.36) and it moved the model the least per dollar (0.20 eval points per $1k).
  • Vendor Beta gave the most value: 0.30 eval points per $1k and the top scorecard (94 / 100), at a mid-range price.
  • Vendor Gamma had the highest quality (κ 0.80, gold accuracy ~90%) but cost the most per accepted task ($6.35) and hit its daily target least often. Useful for the hardest tasks, not the bulk.
  • Beta and Gamma's gold accuracy overlap once you show the 95% intervals. Agreement and eval lift are what separate them.
  • One annotator was flagged as likely low effort: 46% gold accuracy at a quarter of the vendor's typical time per task.

Recommendation

Give most of the next batch to Beta, route the hardest tasks to Gamma, and keep Alpha on a smaller probation share. Before that, Alpha should remove or retrain the flagged annotator, re-audit their work, and join a calibration session. Raise Alpha's gold rate and re-decide after week one.

Limits

  • All data is simulated, including the model-eval results. I built it to practice the decision, not to report real vendor performance.
  • One eval run per vendor; a real decision would need repeated runs before trusting a ~1-point lift.
  • The scorecard weights are a choice. Changing them can change the ranking, which is why the weights are stated up front.
